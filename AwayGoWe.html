8 Worthwhile Things to Do in Portovenere, Italy in One Day

David Baxter October 23, 2026

Portovenere, Italy has just about everything we love in a small coastal town. Colorful waterfront buildings, narrow medieval streets, historic churches, and some pretty spectacular views of the Ligurian Sea.

And the best part? You can experience most of it in a single afternoon!

Lori and I visited Portovenere with our boys on a Saturday in mid-October, taking the scenic ferry from La Spezia.

The weather was looking a bit questionable, but it held out long enough for us to enjoy a wonderful four to five hours exploring this unique and lovely corner of Leguria.

We wandered the waterfront and backstreets, visited centuries-old churches, climbed to a hilltop castle, and soaked up some breathtaking coastal scenery.

Despite being a Saturday, the crowds were manageable and subdued (in contrast to our previous Saturday in Vernazza).

We could have definitely spent more time hiking and exploring nearby Palmaria Island, but even a half-day in Portovenere was well worth the trip here.

Here are 8 worthwhile things to do in Portovenere, Italy, based on our own experience, plus a few things we’d consider next time and practical advice for planning your visit.

Why Visit Portovenere, Italy?

Portovenere certainly has a lot going for it. It’s compact, walkable, packed with history, and surrounded by some pretty darn striking coastal scenery.

The town sits at the entrance to the Gulf of La Spezia (also known as the Gulf of Poets), with colorful buildings lining the waterfront and steep hills rising behind the historic center.

Across a narrow stretch of water lies Palmaria Island, while a bit farther up the coast are the famous five villages of Cinque Terre.

(Psst…If you’re still deciding which Cinque Terre villages to prioritize—or where to stay—we’ve compared all five in our companion guide: Which Cinque Terre Village Is Best for You?)

Portovenere isn’t one of the five Cinque Terre villages, although they share a UNESCO World Heritage designation along with the nearby islands.

What perhaps impressed us most of all was how much variety we found in such a compact area. Within a relatively short walk, we hopped from a colorful seaside promenade to a medieval church overlooking the sea, a rocky swimming cove, and an imposing hilltop fortress.

We were also pleasantly surprised by the atmosphere. Despite visiting on a Saturday, the town felt relaxed and subdued in mid-October. There were plenty of visitors, but nothing approaching the crush of people we’d encountered in some of the Cinque Terre villages.

Would we expect the same in July or August? Perhaps not.

Still, Portovenere struck us as a particularly rewarding place to spend a day, especially if you’re staying in La Spezia or exploring this stretch of the Italian Riviera.

8 Worthwhile Things to Do in Portovenere, Italy

① See Portovenere from the Sea

By far, one of the top things to do in Portovenere that we’d recommend is approaching the town from the water.

We took the passenger ferry from La Spezia, and the roughly 30-minute journey ended up being one of the highlights of our day. The ferry ride offered great views across the Gulf of Poets, with its hills, coastal villages, and many small boats moving across the water on this particular Saturday.

And as we approached Portovenere, we got to appreciate the town’s colorful waterfront buildings from the perspective it seemed like it was meant to be appreciated from. It’s a place that seems made to be seen from the sea.

You don’t necessarily need to book an expensive boat tour to enjoy the experience, either. If you’re staying in La Spezia, the regular passenger ferry combines transportation with sightseeing.

That said, if the weather had been more reliable for us, we would have certainly entertained other reasons to spend more time out on the water around Portovenere (of which there are no shortage of options!).

Boat Tours, Sailing Trips & Sunset Cruises

Depending on the operator, excursions may include small-group boat trips, sailing experiences, swimming stops, cruises around Palmaria Island, and sunset outings with an aperitivo.

If we were returning with more time, we’d seriously consider a boat excursion around the Gulf of Poets, particularly one that included swimming or exploring the islands.

✪ Our Advice If you’re already taking the ferry from La Spezia, don’t feel obligated to book another boat experience unless there’s something specific you want to do. We were more than happy with the views from the regular ferry.

But if you’re visiting by bus or car, a boat tour could offer a completely different perspective on Portovenere and its spectacular setting.

✪ Traveler Tip Public ferries operate on seasonal schedules and can be affected by sea conditions. Check the official ferry timetable before making your plans, particularly outside the main tourist season.

② Wander Portovenere’s Old Town & Colorful Waterfront

Of course, one of the best things to do in Portovenere doesn’t require an admission ticket or much planning.

Just a desire to explore.

The historic center is wonderfully compact, with colorful buildings lining the waterfront and narrow streets and passageways tucked behind the harbor.

Along the promenade, things feel bright and open, with fishing boats, ferries, cafés, and views across the water. But step through one of the passageways leading into the old town, and things take on a different vibe.

Here, tall stone buildings, narrow pedestrian lanes, ancient archways, and little shops create a much older, more enclosed atmosphere.

We enjoyed both sides of Portovenere.

Lori and I initially followed the waterfront toward the Church of San Pietro at the far end of town. Later, we explored the backstreets and worked our way through the historic center toward the main square.

Before catching our return ferry, we also took a leisurely walk along the waterfront in the opposite direction, toward La Spezia. It gave us yet another perspective on town and the harbor and was a pleasant way to spend our remaining time.

For such a small place, Portovenere has a surprising number of interesting corners. We wouldn’t overthink this part of the visit. Just leave yourself some time to wander without a particular destination in mind.

③ Visit the Iconic Church of San Pietro

Of all the sights we visited in Portovenere, the Church of San Pietro might be the most unique.

Perched on a rocky promontory at the far end of town, this centuries-old church occupies one of the most extraordinary coastal settings we’ve encountered in Italy.

It’s impressive enough from a distance, but walking up toward the church and exploring the surrounding terraces gave us an entirely different appreciation for its location.

We happened to arrive during a wedding procession.

As we approached, the church bells were chiming the familiar melody of Here Comes the Bride, while the bride and her escort slowly made their way up the long stone staircase toward the church.

Once we had an opportunity to explore, we climbed to the upper areas around the church and enjoyed memorable views looking out to sea.

The church itself is also remarkable, with its distinctive striped stone facade and compact medieval architecture appearing to emerge right out of the rocky headland beneath it.

Even if you aren’t particularly interested in historic churches, we think San Pietro is an essential stop. And if you only have time for one of Portovenere’s churches, this would probably be our first choice.

✪ Traveler Tip Allow time to explore the different viewpoints around San Pietro rather than simply walking up to the church, taking a photograph, and leaving. Remember that it’s an active religious site, so pay attention to clothing advisory signs and know that access may be limited during ceremonies.

④ Explore Byron’s Grotto & the Rocky Coastline

Just a short walk from San Pietro is another of Portovenere’s most impressive natural attractions: Byron’s Grotto.

Also known as Grotta Byron, this picturesque rocky inlet is tucked away between steep cliffs, with Doria Castle perched dramatically above it.

The grotto takes its name from the English Romantic poet Lord Byron, who found inspiration along this coastline during the 19th century.

These days, the grotto and surrounding rocks attract visitors looking to take in the scenery, snap photos, and, when conditions allow, take a dip in the water.

But keep in mind, swimming here isn’t quite the same as wading into a sandy beach. The shoreline is rocky, access can be uneven, and sea conditions can change quickly.

Even if you have no intention of swimming, Byron’s Grotto is well worth the stop. We think the scenery alone makes it one of Portovenere’s most rewarding attractions.

✪ Our Advice If you’re visiting during the warmer months, bring swimsuits and water shoes. Just be mindful of sea conditions and only enter the water where access is permitted and safe.

⑤ Climb to Doria Castle for the Views

Guarding over Portovenere’s historic center stands Doria Castle, an imposing stone fortress that dominates the town’s skyline.

The castle dates largely to the period of Genoese control and occupies a commanding position above the coastline.

Reaching it involves a short uphill walk through the upper part of town, but we think the climb is worth making even if you don’t intend to go inside (which is what we did).

Admission isn’t a lot (€5 per adult during our visit), but we’ve been visiting quite a few castles and forts recently, and we weren’t especially interested in exploring another one that afternoon.

Instead, we were content to appreciate the exterior and surrounding viewpoints.

Don’t Miss the Church of San Lorenzo

While you’re exploring the upper part of Portovenere, we also recommend stepping inside the nearby Church of San Lorenzo.

Dating to the 12th century, it doesn’t have the dramatic seaside setting of San Pietro, but we found its dark, ancient-feeling interior quite atmospheric.

We didn’t spend long inside, but it was a quick stop that stuck with us. And since you’re already up near the castle anyway, it’s worth a peek.

✪ Our Take Doria Castle is worth walking up to, even if you skip the interior. Just remember that the views from inside the paid castle grounds may differ from the publicly accessible viewpoints we experienced.

⑥ Hike the Scenic Trails Above Portovenere

One thing we wish we’d made more time for in Portovenere was hiking.

The town sits within a spectacular stretch of protected coastline, with trails climbing into the hills above the harbor and continuing toward the surrounding villages.

Much of this area forms part of the Parco Naturale Regionale di Porto Venere, or Porto Venere Regional Natural Park.

We’d already gotten a taste of the landscape while walking up toward Doria Castle, but our original plans included spending more time on the trails.

But, we’d completed a substantial hike through Cinque Terre the previous day, and our legs weren’t begging for another 20k-30k-step day. The uncertain weather didn’t help matters either.

So we decided to take things easier and devote our remaining time to exploring town.

Still, if you enjoy hiking, we’d strongly recommend looking into the park’s scenic routes. Here’s what we drummed up in our prior research (and what we plan to do next time):

Portovenere to Campiglia: The AV5T Trail

One of the best-known routes connects Portovenere with Campiglia along the Alta Via delle Cinque Terre (AV5T), a longer high-level trail network extending toward Cinque Terre.

The route climbs behind Doria Castle and into the hills, offering coastal viewpoints and Mediterranean scenery along the way.

The hike to Campiglia is said to take approximately two hours one way and is considered moderately difficult. Expect sustained climbing, uneven terrain, and sections exposed to the sun.

If you’re short on time, walking only part of the route and returning to Portovenere sounds like it could be a good alternative, provided you’re comfortable with the terrain.

We’d love to return and spend more time exploring these trails. After completing our Cinque Terre Blue Trail hike the day before, we came away convinced that some of the best experiences along this coast happen away from the busy waterfronts.

▸ See the official Porto Venere Regional Natural Park trail information

✪ Traveler Tip Wear proper hiking shoes, carry water, and check current trail conditions. These are mountain-style hiking paths, not necessarily casual walking trails.

⑦ Explore Palmaria Island

Directly across the narrow channel from Portovenere lies Palmaria Island, a protected island known for its rugged coastline, hiking trails, secluded coves, and wonderful views back toward the mainland.

Palmaria was originally part of our day-trip plan.

We’d hoped to take the short boat crossing from Portovenere and hike around the island. But between our big Cinque Terre hiking day the previous afternoon and the increasingly threatening weather, we ultimately decided against it.

In hindsight, we think that was the right decision for the day. Still, Palmaria remains near the top of our list for a return trip.

Hiking on Palmaria Island

One of Palmaria’s main attractions is its network of hiking trails.

The island’s principal circuit, known as the Periplo dell’Isola, passes through Mediterranean vegetation and offers views across the gulf, surrounding islands, and Portovenere itself.

The regional park lists the circuit as a moderate hike taking approximately 3.5 hours, with several climbs and descents. That makes it a proper half-day outing rather than a quick stroll after lunch.

Depending on the season, visitors can arrange passenger-boat crossings from Portovenere. Check which services and landing points are operating before making your plans.

With only four or five hours in Portovenere, we’d prioritize the town’s main sights. But if you have a full day or an overnight stay, Palmaria becomes a much more realistic addition.

For those more interested in the coastline than hiking, a boat excursion around Palmaria and the neighboring islands may be another worthwhile option.

▸ Read the official Palmaria Island hiking information

✪ Heads Up We didn’t make it onto Palmaria during our visit, so these are researched recommendations rather than a firsthand review of the trails.

⑧ Try Some Delicious Ligurian Focaccia

Italy has no shortage of fantastic food. And while Liguria is well known for pesto, seafood, and other regional specialties, one of our favorite simple pleasures along this coast has been the focaccia.

Ligurian focaccia is a soft, olive-oil-rich flatbread with a lightly crisp exterior. It’s delicious on its own and makes an excellent base for sandwiches.

During our visit, we stopped near Portovenere’s main square and enjoyed some tasty focaccia sandwiches.

Portovenere has a variety of cafés, bakeries, and restaurants around the waterfront and historic center. Opting for grabbing something quick and tasty rather than doing a sit-down made a lot of sense for our outing and left more time for sightseeing.

Of course, if you’re staying overnight or simply want to linger over lunch, a sit-down meal featuring Ligurian seafood or pasta with pesto might be a better choice.

For us, the focaccia sandwiches hit the spot.

✪ Our Advice? Don’t feel obligated to book a fancy waterfront restaurant to enjoy Portovenere’s food scene. A good focaccia sandwich from a local bakery can be a memorable—and relatively affordable—part of your visit.

Planning Your Visit to Portovenere

Portovenere is straightforward to explore independently, but there are a few things worth knowing before you go, particularly regarding transportation and how much time to allow.

How to Get to Portovenere from La Spezia

For most independent travelers, La Spezia is the most convenient starting point. It’s well connected by train and offers both bus and ferry services to Portovenere.

We took the ferry and would happily choose the same option again, weather permitting.

Ferry from La Spezia

The passenger ferry is our recommended way to reach Portovenere. The journey took around 30 minutes and offered great views across the Gulf of Poets.

Services are operated by Navigazione Golfo dei Poeti, with routes connecting La Spezia, Portovenere, and other destinations around the gulf.

Ferries generally operate through the main tourist season, with some additional services into late fall. Exact schedules change annually, and sailings can be affected by sea conditions.

▸ Check official ferry timetables and fares

Bus from La Spezia

If the ferry isn’t running (or simply isn’t your thing), no worries! Portovenere is also accessible by public bus.

ATC operates routes connecting La Spezia with Portovenere, including services numbered 11 and P. Travel time is said to typically take around 30 minutes, depending on traffic and where you board.

The bus is particularly useful outside ferry operating periods or when the weather is unsettled.

▸ ATC La Spezia public transportation information

Can you drive? Yes, although parking can be limited, especially during busy periods. We’d generally favor public transportation for a day trip like this.

Also worth noting, is that Portovenere has no train station. If you’re arriving by rail, you’ll need to continue from La Spezia by ferry, bus, or road.

Can You Visit Portovenere from Cinque Terre?

Yes, you can! We think Portovenere would make a great addition to a trip along this stretch of the Ligurian coast, although getting there isn’t quite as straightforward as hopping on the local Cinque Terre train.

During the main tourist season, ferries connect Portovenere with several of the five villages, including Riomaggiore, Manarola, Vernazza, and Monterosso. Services and stops vary by season.

Outside ferry operating periods, you’ll generally need to travel via La Spezia.

But should you combine Portovenere and Cinque Terre in a single day?

It is possible, if you’re short on time (more so, when the ferries are running in tourist season). Personally, though, we’d recommend allowing separate days if your itinerary permits.

Where to Stay in Portovenere (or La Spezia)

If you’re planning to explore both Portovenere and Cinque Terre, one of the first decisions you’ll need to make is where to base yourself.

We stayed in La Spezia and found it a convenient jumping-off point for visiting both destinations.

Staying in Portovenere

Staying in Portovenere would be our choice for travelers looking for a more atmospheric seaside experience, particularly couples or anyone hoping to enjoy the town after the day-trippers have gone home.

An overnight stay would also give you more flexibility for hiking or visiting Palmaria Island. The main drawback is transportation, particularly outside the ferry season.

Staying in La Spezia

Staying in La Spezia makes more sense for travelers planning several day trips. It offers train connections to Cinque Terre, ferries and buses to Portovenere, and a wider selection of accommodations and services.

▸ Compare La Spezia hotels and apartments

It doesn’t have the same intimate coastal atmosphere, but is an agreeable town in its own right. As a practical base, we found it hard to beat.

✪ Our Take We’d choose La Spezia for a multi-destination sightseeing trip, but Portovenere for a slower seaside stay.

How Much Time Do You Need in Portovenere?

We spent approximately four or five hours exploring Portovenere, which felt like a good amount of time for seeing the historic center and its main attractions without rushing.

Here’s what we’d suggest depending on your available time.

If you have only a half day, don’t worry about trying to squeeze everything in. We think Portovenere is a great place to slow down and just enjoy the atmosphere.

When Is the Best Time to Visit Portovenere?

We visited on a Saturday in mid-October and thought it was a pretty good time to be there. The weather was somewhat unsettled but held out during our visit, and the crowds were manageable.

The next day brought rain, so we were particularly glad we’d taken advantage of the opportunity.

✓ Spring and early fall are appealing for sightseeing and hiking, with generally milder temperatures than midsummer.

✓ Summer offers the best opportunities for swimming, boating, and long days by the water, although you should expect more visitors and some very hot days.

✓ Late fall and winter can be quieter, but ferry services are more limited and the weather rainier and less predictable.

Suggested One-Day Portovenere Itinerary

For a first-time visit, we’d start with the scenic ferry journey from La Spezia, then explore the colorful waterfront and historic center.

Continue toward the far end of town to visit San Pietro and Byron’s Grotto. From there, walk uphill toward Doria Castle, stopping at San Lorenzo before heading back down for lunch.

That covers most of Portovenere’s major sights in a manageable half day.

If you’ve got more time, spend the afternoon walking along the coastline, hiking above town, or exploring Palmaria Island, depending on your interests and the weather.

Just remember to check your return ferry departure if you’re relying on the boat back to La Spezia.

Is Portovenere Worth Visiting?

Portovenere ended up being one of the nicest surprises of our time exploring the Ligurian coast.

We weren’t expecting not to like it, exactly. We just didn’t realize how much history, atmosphere, and wonderful scenery would be packed into this compact little town.

The ferry from La Spezia was a highlight in itself, while San Pietro, Byron’s Grotto, Doria Castle, and the old town offered more than enough to make the day worthwhile.

However, next time, we’d bring our swimsuits, make more time for hiking, and hopefully get out to Palmaria Island.

But even with just four or five hours, there were more than enough things to do in Portovenere to make it well worth the trip!
