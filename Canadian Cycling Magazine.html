Paul Seixas has better things to do than talk contracts in Singapore

The 20-year-old is (trying) to some post-season downtime in Asia, with his future beyond 2027 still very much up in the air

Matt HansenPublished November 8, 2026

Sirotti

Paul Seixas isn’t in Singapore to talk about his future. He’s there to enjoy himself. And if anyone wants to discuss his next contract, they should probs wait until he’s back from his paid vacation. Well, technically that free holiday comes with a catch–he has to race…erm..participate in the The Tour de France EFGH Singapore Criterium.

Not only does he get a free trip, but apparently a sizable start fee, along with a bunch of other big names–including another young star, Isaac Del Toro. But that also means a bunch of publicity stuff-interviews, as well as riding around in circles pretending to be racing.

The French sensation, fresh off a spectacular season capped by victory at the Il Lombardia, is in Asia for a couple of post-season crits. He’ll race in Singapore on Sunday before heading to Saitama, Japan, next week. For now, though, checking out the local scene, and chilling takes priority over negotiations. (He clearly did the same when he raced here in Canada too. He recently did an interview back home in France in a smashing Montreal Expos jacket! He must have hit the shops when in town for the road worlds.)

The ongoing clash with media

In Singapore, he was asked by L’Équipe whether he’d used the break to think about his future beyond his current Decathlon-CMA CGM deal, which expires at the end of 2027. The young man was having none of it. Not one bit.

“I’m on vacation. If I start discussing contracts here, I think I’m going to go crazy,” he said.
The (just turned) 20-year-old added that there’s no rush to make a decision, even as speculation about his next move continues to swirl.

Will Seixas stay or will he go now

“Will I stay? Will I not stay? For now, there’s no certainty,” he said. Some of the reports and social media gossip have even made him laugh. Especially when he comes across intel about his own future that he hadn’t heard before. It’s not the first time–and almost certainly not the last time, he will be asked about this. In fact, last week he even said he’s getting just a smidge fed up with it all.

“It’s going to become more and more oppressive,” Seixas said, according to RMC. “I can’t make a decision at the last minute either.”

He also said that cashola won’t necessarily be the deciding factor, either. Seixas says he wants to find an environment where he feels comfortable and gets along with the people around him.

“I’m looking for a feeling, a connection. I really want to feel in tune with the team, with people I like. I think that’s the most important thing.”

If he stays there won’t be trouble

But he has noticed that his current team, Decathlon CMA CGM has been beefing up the team. They have added riders Ben O’Connor, Pavel Sivakov and Laurens De Plus to add depth–and firepower, to help him at the big races.

There’s also plenty to feel good about after a remarkable campaign. Seixas said he finished the season in excellent physical and mental condition, giving him confidence heading into 2027. His Lombardy win, his first Monument victory, was an especially emotional way to sign off.

With no more racing objectives immediately on the horizon, he could finally enjoy the moment, he said.

For now, the contract talk can wait. Singapore has some sightseeing to offer.

Stairway to heaven: Thibau Nys runs to another European title

The Belgian outgunned his compatriot in a thrilling final-round battle, using a perfectly timed attack on the 99-step climb to secure white-and-blue jersey

Matt HansenPublished November 8, 2026

Thibau Nys is European champion again. The Belgian ace successfully defended his elite men’s cyclocross title in Zeddam on Sunday, but he defo had to work for it. His teammate for the day, compatriot Joran Wyseure, pushed him all the way to the final lap. Nys, who has already dominated ‘cross–he began a bit later due to the fact that he does a proper WorldTour season, has clearly reinforced his role as the third dude in the New Big Three. His fellow Big Three’ers, Mathieu van der Poel and Wout van Aert are still on vacation, but they should be joining him soon.

The race was super-fast from the start. Dutch rider Mees Hendrix got the holeshot. Several of the big names, including Nys and Joris Nieuwenhuis, were initially further back, but the fast course kept the field relatively close together. And don’t forget, each lap of the course – designed by former world champion Erwin Verwecken – had around 99 steps of running. Plus, it was super undulating with some tough little climbs. A proper leg-burner.

Nys goes clear (sorta)

Nys eventually shot his shot after around 20 minutes. He took off, but then Swiss rider Kevin Kuhn and Wyseure bridged across. The trio established themselves at the front. But Nys continued to apply pressure. Kuhn was eventually dropped, leaving the two Belgians to fight for gold. And sure, they are teammates…but not really. Remember, championship jersey > bro hug.

Meanwhile, Nieuwenhuis tried to join the party, while Britain’s Cameron Mason produced an impressive recovery to close in on the leaders. Nice to see Mason in the mix.

With two laps remaining, the gap was still small enough to keep the chasing group interested. But stuff was still pretty close.

Then he really goes clear

But the decisive move came on the final lap. Nys hit the 99-step staircase with a huge acceleration, immediately opening daylight on Wyseure. The younger Belgian had no answer as the defending champion powered away to take his second consecutive European title.

Wyseure settled for silver after a terrific ride. And then Niels Vandeputte completed an all-Belgian podium by winning the sprint for bronze ahead of Nieuwenhuis and Kuhn.

There was no drama like the women’s race the day before when Lucinda Brand was given the win after being outsprinted by Maria Schreiber. The one-person jury awarded Brand the dub after seeing a sprint deviation that the rest of the world didn’t quite catch.
