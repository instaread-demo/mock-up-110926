Bo Bice

Bo Bice

Southern rock singer and songwriter Harold Elwin "Bo" Bice Jr. of Alabama (1975- ) is best known for finishing as the runner-up in the fourth season of American Idol, behind Carrie Underwood. With his distinct blend of southern rock, blues, jazz, and gospel influences, he has since released three solo albums, one of which has been certified “gold” by the Recording Industry Association of America (RIAA). He has contributed to multiple significant musical projects, including performing the lead single from the film Blades of Glory and serving as the lead singer for Blood, Sweat & Tears from 2013 to 2018. Throughout his career, he has garnered a reputation as a philanthropic performer, particularly for his frequent performances for U.S. service members stationed overseas.

Bice was born on November 1, 1975, in Huntsville, Madison County to Nancy Downes and Elwin Bice. From the age of nine, he was raised by his mother and stepfather, Earl Downes. Bice spent his earliest years in Somerville, Morgan County, but his family moved to Covington, Georgia, soon after. Throughout his childhood, he was always surrounded by family members who loved singing and playing various instruments, which influenced his early development into a singer and musician. During his teenage years, Bice’s family moved to London for his stepfather’s career at Coca-Cola. While attending London Central High School, Bice started his first band, Spinning Jenny. After moving back to Alabama and earning his GED, Bice formed the band Purge, his first professional band, at 17 years old. With Purge, he recorded the album Exgratia in 1996. In the ensuing years, he studied music at Calhoun Community College in Tanner, Limestone County and played frequently at nightclubs with his band, SugarMoney (also known by other variations).

In 2004, Bice auditioned for the fourth season of American Idol in Orlando, Florida. At the time, he was largely unknown as a performer beyond his hometown. For his audition Bice displayed his southern rock and blues influences with a cover of The Allman Brothers Band’s “Whipping Post.” After moving past the audition round, Bice performed hits such as “Remedy” by The Black Crowes, “Free Bird” by Lynyrd Skynyrd (which landed him in the Bottom 2 for the first and only time in the competition), “Vehicle” by The Ides of March, and an a cappella version of “In a Dream” by Badlands (often considered his signature performance on the show). Bice maintained residency in Helena, Shelby County, during the competition, and community watch parties were held in Huntsville, Somerville, and Birmingham, Jefferson County. In anticipation of the season 4 finale, then-Alabama governor Bob Riley declared May 24, 2005, “Bo Bice Day” in the state of Alabama to encourage American Idol viewers to vote for Bice to win the competition.

Bice was considered a front-runner throughout the fourth season of American Idol, and he finished the competition in second place behind Carrie Underwood. For the finale, Bice and Underwood both performed renditions of “Inside Your Heaven,” an original song written for the season. After the finale, Bice married Caroline Fisher on June 15, 2005. The couple had four children and resided in Alabama, Georgia, and Florida at various times before divorcing in 2026. On June 21, 2005, Bice released his own version of “Inside Your Heaven (a week after Underwood released her version), and Bice’s version reached number two on the Billboard Hot 100 during the week of July 9, 2005. Although “Inside Your Heaven” is Bice’s only Top 10 Hit to date, his releases have still garnered much public attention. In December 2005, Bice released The Real Thing, his first solo album, debuting at number four on the Billboard 200, largely thanks to his success on American Idol. It was eventually certified gold by the RIAA for selling 500,000 copies.

Just as Bice’s career started to flourish, medical problems began to interfere with his touring and recording schedules. While touring at the end of 2005 and the beginning of 2006, Bice was hospitalized 20 times due to complications from an intestinal blockage and ensuing surgeries. Bice was also dropped from his label, RCA, following the release of his first album. After recovering from his medical ailments and starting his own label, Sugar Money, Bice released his second solo album See the Light in 2007 and his third solo album 3 in 2010. While the albums didn’t chart as well and peaked at number 150 and number 154, respectively, on the Billboard 200, Bice’s career also expanded to new areas.

A few months before releasing See the Light, Bice recorded “Blades of Glory,” the lead single on the soundtrack for the film of the same name. In 2013, Bice became the lead singer of Blood, Sweat & Tears, a long-running and critically acclaimed jazz-rock ensemble group known for blending various musical styles, largely thanks to his American Idol performance of the group’s signature song “Spinning Wheel.” Bice fronted the group, performing their hits from the 1960s and 1970s, until 2018. In 2023, Bice was cast in a three-month run of the Rock of Ages musical in Florida.

Bice also became a regular performer for charitable endeavors, namely for the U.S. Armed Forces. In 2008, Bice spent a week in Kuwait and Afghanistan performing for American service members. On New Year's Eve in 2010, Bice performed for military personnel stationed in Guantanamo Bay, Cuba. In 2011, Bice also contributed a speaking role and rendition of the Confederate marching song “The Bonnie Blue Flag” to Civil War Songs & Stories, a PBS documentary. Some of his other charitable performances include a 2005 benefit concert for victims of Hurricane Katrina and a 2018 benefit concert for the Helena Miracle League, an Alabama-based baseball league for children and young adults with disabilities.

Bice often donates his profits to various relief programs. For example, he donated the revenue from his song “Long Road Back,” the lead single off his album 3, to flood relief programs following the record-breaking Nashville flood in May 2010. When he became the first person to win the grand prize of $50,000 on the celebrity version of the game show “Don’t Forget the Lyrics,” Bice donated the grand prize to MusiCares, a charity that supports basic financial and health needs for people in the music industry.

Bice has remained an active performer throughout the 2020s and released two singles in 2024: a cover of “Ramblin’ Gamblin’ Man” by Bob Seger and an original song entitled “Born of Heroes’ Blood,” which won a Hollywood Independent Music Award. For several months in 2025, Bice was lead singer for the Artimus Pyle Band (APB), a tribute to the band Lynyrd Skynyrd for whom Pyle played drums in its prime years in the 1970s and in later renditions.
