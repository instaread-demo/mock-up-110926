Two Italians Send an Adam Ondra 5.15a

Filip Schenk and Gio Placci clip the chains on Naturalmente in Italy

Aaron PardyPublished November 8, 2026

Listen to audio version of this article
Sponsored by

Instaread

Gio Placci and Filip Schenk recently redpointed Naturalmente 5.15a (9a+) at the San Rocchino sector in Camiore, Italy. Opened by Adam Ondra in 2017, the five-star route works its way up a slightly overhanging wall on small crimps, underclings, and poor feet. Ondra got it done in three days of effort.

Placci and Schenk’s dual send follows their recent ascent of another Ondra route, Goldrake 5.15a in Lombardy, Italy. The historic route was Italy’s first of the grade, although subsequent ascensionists – as well as Ondra – have questioned whether it is indeed 5.14d. For Naturalmente, however, Ondra believed the route to be high in the grade for 5.15a.

“Naturalmente was the second goal of the trip,” said Schenk on Instagram about Naturalmente. “I always wanted to visit Camaiore, so I took the opportunity and it didn’t disappoint! The route consists of three crux boulders with some decent rests in between. A lot of bad footholds and big moves to underclings make this route unique! For the first time, I experienced the same pump in my legs as in my forearms, due to the bad feet and the long rests.

“The flash attempt ended at the hardest crux at the beginning, which felt very low-percentage for me, even in the attempts afterwards. A bit disappointed about that, because I think I could have had a good fight after that!”

“Such an incredible route,” added Placci on Instagram. “It was my first time in the crag of San Rocchino and it was worth the visit, the climbing there is super cool and the people super welcoming. The routes really is five stars but also doesn’t suit my style, bad foot and big moves on good holds. It was a real battle for me to send it in the three days I was there.”

In September this year, Schenk achieved his hardest sport climbing flash ever with his ascent of Vivi si Muore 5.14d at La Stazione in Piedmont, Italy. He has climbed many hard routes, including Stefano Ghisolfi’s Erebor 5.15b in Arco. Placci also flashed his hardest sport climb this year in August with his send of The Famous Gem 5.14d in Briançon, France. He climbed Erebor 5.15b in 2024. He has climbed at least 2o routes 5.14d or harder according to his 8a.nu page.

Hamish McArthur Flashes Silent Menace 5.14c in Squamish

He's the first climber to flash the bouldery Sonnie Trotter and Ben Harnden line

Aaron PardyPublished November 8, 2026

Hamish McArthur recently flashed Silent Menace 5.14c (8c+) in Squamish, Canada. Located in the Cacodemon boulders not far from Dreamcatcher 5.14d and Event Horizon V16, Silent Menace works its way up a steep granite face. The climb starts with jump from a boulder and then moves up small edges requiring strong fingers and good technique.

Silent Menace was first opened by Sonnie Trotter at a grade of 5.14a. Later, a large flake broke off the route (which you can watch in a video below). Ben Harnden made the first post-break ascent, upgrading it to 5.14c and suggesting a new name – Smell the Glove.

The route has since been repeated by top climbers like Jamie Finlayson and Ethan Salvo, although this is the first known flash of the notoriously bouldery route.

“[Silent Menace ~5.14c] flash … hell yeah!” said McArthur on Instagram. “A big thanks to Quinn [Mason] for filming, ticking, belaying and colour grading. I love it when climbing becomes a team sport. [Sonnie Trotter and Ben Harnden] thanks for the double FA you guys are the best.”

We talked with Harnden about Silent Menace in an interview in 2024. “I could do the huge jump to the jug the start, but just barely,” he said of the original pre-break climb. “On one go, I got to the very last hold on the lip near the chains and then fell. I was psyched! I felt like it was going to be my first 5.14a. I came back a week later and the jug had been ripped off. Someone had a near miss with the hold breaking – you can watch it on YouTube. The climber was fine and luckily his belayer was too.

“I still really wanted to climb that pitch. I was so close to the 5.14a. But [with the hold break] it went from possible to impossible. I spent half a summer working on the moves trying to climb through the new low crux.  I slowly discovered that you could still crimp and jump through it, but it bumped the beginning boulder from V8 to V12. After finally climbing it, I renamed the route Smell the Glove because [the hold break] changed the grade from a 5.14a to a 5.14c. It was a cool route to open up the mind and persevere on something quite hard and see through to the end. The whole time I could see Dreamcatcher lurking there through my peripherals – it took a few more seasons to get to that.”

McArthur, 24, adds Silent Menace to many of his other hard Squamish climbs. Earlier this year he clipped the chains of Spirit Quest 5.14d, one of Canada’s hardest sport routes. He also made first ascents of Flower of Sulphur V16 and Tilde V15. Over the period of a couple weeks in 2025, he repeated No One Mourns the Wicked V17 in Thunder Ridge, Colorado and Megatron V17 in Eldorado Canyon, Colorado.
