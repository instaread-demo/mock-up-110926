Winix 5530 review

Can this new model from Winix match the value of the now discontinued Winix 5500-2?

The 5530 is a new air purifier from Winix and part of their new generation of mid-sized models with app connectivity and smart features.

It comes with separate HEPA and carbon filters to deal with both particles and gases, and it also has a removable pre-filter that catches larger particles like pet hair and lint.

But can this new model deliver better air cleaning performance than the 5510 and the 5520?

To find out, I bought one with my own money and spent months using at home in order to test its air cleaning performance, noise generation, energy consumption, annual running costs, and day-to-day user experience.

The overall score for the Winix 5530 incorporates first-hand data results from our battery of tests, as well as our ratings for customer service, brand transparency, and design & UX. Check out the full scorecard below to see the category breakdown.

The pros and cons of the Winix 5530

A summary of our top-level findings after testing this air purifier, following our methodology.

$219.99 AT AMAZON

“As our test data shows, this new white model from Winix is very similar to the Winix 5510 and 5520… Not just in looks but also likely on the inside, as we encountered some minor differences in energy consumption and sound generation.

And when we compare performance to other mid-sized air purifiers, the 5530 ranks well – – offering the same top speed power as the popular Vital 200S from Levoit and higher performance than the Blueair 311i Max.”

CONS

It can get pretty loud at top speed, reaching 63.1 decibels.
If you set it to auto mode, it will automatically switch to sleep mode when it detects darkness. There’s no way to disable this.
The app is pretty basic compared to what you get from other brands like Levoit and Philips.

PROS

It offers good performance at top speed considering its price.
The filters are separate, so you won’t need to throw away a True HEPA filter that still has life in it once the carbon runs out.
The pre-filter is removable and easy to clean. Plus, it’s washable too.
This is subjective, but I know a lot of people will like the white as it will fit nicely in most homes
It has PlasmaWave, which is a bipolar ionization feature that can be disabled if you prefer.

Filtration technology

True HEPA filtration with a removable pre-filter and activated carbon pellets.

Just like the 5510 and 5520, the Winix 5530 comes pre-filter that traps large particles, a True HEPA filter that removes the smallest of particles, and an activated carbon filter that adsorbs odors and gases.

The pre-filter is removable and washable, and you need to remember to clean it every 1-2 weeks – especially if you have a pet at home.

The 5530 uses Winix’s Type Q filters, which include one True HEPA filter and one activated carbon filter. The set has a list price of $79.99 but you will often find it discounted on places like Amazon.

There are many generic filters available for the 5530. The biggest difference I’ve found with genuine filters is in the quality/quantity of carbon.

In a world where most brands have opted for bonded, all-in-one filters, I really appreciate that Winix still separates their particle filter from the gas filter.

This way, you will be able to replace the gas filter once the carbon is full, without having to also throw away a particle filter that still has life in it.

Design, controls and app features

A white, rectangular air purifier that could fit most bedrooms and living rooms.

At a glance, the 5530 reminds me of the Winix C535 – although the C535 is a little bigger.

But unlike the C535, the 5530 connects to the Winix app, which allows you to control it remotely, set timers and keep track of the filter lifespan.

You get buttons to adjust modes and fan speeds, and to enable (or disable) PlasmaWave.

It has a gas sensor that informs the auto mode and the air quality indicator light at the front of the device that changes from blue to red.

This new generation of mid-sized units from Winix all have the same clean and understated look.

I really like the black in the 5510 and the 5520 but I know a lot of people will prefer a white unit, and I guess Winix knows that too.

Air cleaning performance

We tested the Winix 5530 in our 728-cubic-ft test room, measuring how quickly it removed PM1 particles from the air after we filled the space with incense smoke.

$219.99 AT AMAZON

When running at top speed, our PurpleAir Zen sensor shows that the 5530 needed 24 minutes to reach PM1 zero.

Based on the natural ventilation of our test room, we estimate a PM1 CADR of 165 cfm. This makes the Winix 5530 powerful enough to provide five air changes per hour in small rooms up to 372 sq. ft. (or 34.5 m2).

This is the same time it took both the Winix 5510 and 5520, and it compares well with other mid-sized models I have tested:

Based on this result and considering its list price of $179.99, the 5530 offers a cost per CADR of 72 cents:

BUT results at top speed won’t tell us the full story… Air purifiers running at full power can be as noisy as a vacuum cleaner, and nobody wants a vacuum cleaner running in the background 24/7.

That is why we take the time to measure how much noise air purifiers generate at each fan speed, so we can put performance into context.

Sound levels

The sound levels of the Winix range from 40.2 dBA to 63.1 dBA, so when running at top speed it’s as loud as a dryer.

As always, I used our BAFX sound meter to measure how much sound the Winix 5530 generates at each of its fan speeds at 3 ft away:

Reaching 63.1 dBA at top speed, the 5530 is one of the loudest medium sized models I tested. The only model in the benchmark louder is the Coway Airmega 200M and its 64.3 dBA.

Running at speed 2, it reached 44.8 dBA and cleared our test room in 46 minutes. This is the same result of the Levoit Vital 200S – but the Vital 200S did this at quieter operation (41.9 dBA).

Based on the natural ventilation of our test room, I estimate a PM1 CADR of 128 cfm at speed 2, which can clean the air five times per hour in spaces up to 192 sq. ft. (or 17 m²).

The 5530 is the third new mid-szed air purifier released by Winix in the past 12 months, after the 5520 and the 5510.

Our test data shows that all three models offer the same air cleaning power at top speed and consume similar levels of electricity. However, the 5510 is quieter across all fan speeds.

If you’re choosing for a Winix for your bedroom or a space where sound matters, then I recommend you go for the Winix 5510 instead.

Long-term running costs

Running a Winix 5530 will cost you $167 per year, combining energy and filter replacement costs.

We used our energy meter to record how much electricity this Winix model pulls at each fan speed and while on standby.

At top speed, the 5530 draws 54.16 watts, so if you were to leave it running at top speed 24/7 for one year, it will add $54.16 to your energy bill, based on the latest electricity prices in the U.S. as of November 2026.

This Winix unit is one of the most power-hungry mid-sized air purifiers I’ve tested. But energy consumption isn’t the only cost to consider, so let’s look at replacement filters.

According to Winix, you need to change the filter in your 5300 every 12 months. The original filter is available for $79.99, so you can expect to spend $79.99 each year on filter replacements alone.

I added this to the energy costs and can tell you that you need to budget $167 each year to keep your Winix 5530 running 24/7 in your home:

This places the 5530 bang on in the middle of the chart, in between the new Coway Mighty2 and the PC fan-powered AirFanta 3Pro.

When you add the $179.99 list price to the yearly running cost, you will need to invest $346.99 in the first year of owning a Winix 5530.

This makes the 5530 the second cheapest mid-sized air purifier to buy and run in the first year of ownership – – second only to the Levoit Vital 200S and its cheaper filter replacements.

Bottom line: Should you get a Winix 5530?

If sound is a concern, you should go for the Winix 5510 instead. It costs the same and offers the same air cleaning power at quieter operation.

$219.99 AT AMAZON

As our test data shows, this new white model from Winix offers the same top speed power as the Levoit Vital 200S and better air cleaning performance than the Blueair 311i Max.

And when we compare to other mid-sized Winix models, the 5530 is very similar to the Winix 5510 and 5520 in terms of design and features, as well as air cleaning power.

The biggest difference between these three lies in how much sound they generate – with the 5510 being significantly quieter than the other two, especially at top speed:

So if you like what the Winix 5530 has to offer but you’re shopping for an air purifier for a room where sound matters, then I would consider the Winix 5510 instead.

All in all, with a $180 price tag, I’d say that the Winix 5530 offers good value for your money. In exchange, you’ll get a good performing air purifier with separate filters and app support that won’t cost you an arm and a leg to run.
