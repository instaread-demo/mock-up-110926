31 Ivy League Haircuts For Men That Make Short Hair Look Sharp

ByDiane Newell

The Ivy League is one of those haircuts that works for almost everyone, no matter your age, whether you have thick waves, tight curls, or a head full of silver. It keeps the sides short and leaves enough length on top to sweep, part, or push back, so you get a clean shape that’s easy to style and a little longer than the classic crew cut. Below, you’ll find options that range from classic side parts and soft tapers to textured, bearded, and faded takes on this timeless cut.

1. Wavy Ivy League Haircut for Thick Hair

Thick, wavy hair can be a handful when it grows out, but cut short on the sides with a couple of inches left on top, it suddenly behaves. Your natural wave does most of the styling for you, so a pea-sized dab of matte cream and a quick comb back and to the side is all it takes.

2. Relaxed Ivy League with Gray Sides

If gray is creeping in at your temples, this cut puts it on display in a way that looks deliberate. Keeping the sides short lets the silver play against the darker top, while the loose, slightly undone finish keeps things easygoing. It’s a smart pick if you’d rather spend two minutes on your hair in the morning.

3. Short Ivy League with a Skin Fade

Pairing a skin fade with a short Ivy League top gives you razor-clean edges and just enough length to push to one side. The fade also frames your beard, so everything from your temples to your jawline looks sharp.

4. Ivy League with a Taper

A gentle taper is the classic way to cut an Ivy League, and there’s a reason it has stuck around for decades. The sides shorten gradually instead of dropping off, so the cut grows out smoothly and still looks clean at week four. Sweep the top to one side with a light pomade for a classic finish.

5. Bleached Ivy League with a Textured Top

Going platinum is a gutsy move, and an Ivy League shape keeps it grounded so the hair color can steal the show. The short sides make upkeep simple. Bleached hair is thirstier than it looks, so a purple shampoo once a week and a lightweight conditioner will keep it from turning brassy or straw-like.

6. Ivy League Haircut with a Full Beard

A full beard adds a lot of heft to the lower half of your face, so a neat Ivy League keeps everything in proportion. The short sides flow into your beard line, which gives your face structure instead of a shaggy, overgrown look. Ask your barber to blend your sideburns into the beard so there’s no awkward patch where the two meet.

7. Ivy League with a Tapered Fade

This version of the Ivy League splits the difference between a soft taper and a bold fade, giving you a fresh finish. The top stays short enough to air-dry in minutes, which is a lifesaver on rushed mornings.

8. Ivy League for Silver Hair

Silver hair looks its best with a clean, tailored cut, and the Ivy League gives it a dignified shape. Keeping it short avoids the fluffy, wispy look longer white hair might take on. A bit of light styling cream and a side sweep is plenty.

9. Swept-Back Ivy League with Salt-and-Pepper Sides

Sweeping the top back instead of to the side gives the classic Ivy League a slightly dressier feel. The salt-and-pepper sides add natural dimension, so even a simple cut looks layered and interesting. Work a little medium-hold pomade through damp hair, comb it back, and it’ll stay put through a full day.

10. Wavy Ivy League with Tousled Texture

Not every Ivy League needs to look freshly combed, and this tousled version shows how a little mess can work in your favor. Leaving extra length on top lets your waves move around, which suits that windswept look.

11. Textured Ivy League with Natural Curl

Curly hair and the Ivy League get along better than you’d think, as long as your barber or stylist leaves enough length on top for the curls to form. The tight sides keep your head shape neat while the top does its own thing. Scrunch in a little curl cream while your hair is still damp and let it air-dry.

12. Short Side-Swept Ivy League

This is the haircut for the guy who wants to look sharp without thinking about his hair for more than a minute. The top is short enough to towel-dry and push to one side, and the sides stay neat. It’s especially handy if you’re on the go a lot and don’t have much time for styling.

13. Side-Part Ivy League with a Beard

A deep side part brings a bit of old-school charm to this Ivy League haircut, and pairing it with a short beard keeps it from feeling too buttoned-up. The neat part draws the eye upward while the beard adds weight to your jaw, so your face looks balanced. Choose a soft part line or a shaved one.

14. Piecey Ivy League with Texture

If smooth, combed hair feels too stiff for you, this piecey version breaks things up in a good way. A little matte paste worked through dry hair separates the top into chunky sections, giving it movement and a relaxed attitude. The graying sides stay short and clean, so even with all that texture up top, the look never tips into sloppy.

15. Silver Ivy League Haircut with a Side Part

Gray hair can look washed out when it’s cut without much shape, but a defined side part fixes that right away. The direction of the part gives your gray hair structure and shine, and the short sides keep it from puffing out.

16. Long Ivy League for Thick Hair

If your hair is thick, a little extra length on top gives it somewhere to go besides straight out. This version keeps about two inches up front so the hair can roll back and to the side in a loose wave, while the tapered sides stop it from looking heavy around the ears.

17. Side-Swept Ivy League for Men Over 50

Hair often thins a bit at the crown and temples after 50, and sweeping the top to one side is one of the easiest ways to make it look fuller. The longer front gives you enough length to brush across with some lift, while the shorter sides keep the gray neat. A small amount of thickening cream on damp hair, combed over with your fingers, gives it body.

18. Textured Ivy League with Volume

A little lift at the front makes a big difference, and this version of the Ivy League gives your hair just enough height to look full and healthy. The top is brushed up and back with a soft wave, while the short sides make that volume stand out even more. Try a texturizing spray before you blow-dry, then finish with a light cream to keep the shape without crunch.

19. Ivy League Haircut for Men Over 60

At 60 and beyond, a tidy Ivy League might be the most flattering cut you can get. It keeps white hair looking full and deliberate, and the short sides cut down on the wispy flyaways that tend to show up with age. Grow your beard out to match, and the two together give you a distinguished look that still feels warm and approachable.

20. Side-Parted Ivy League with a Low Taper

Straight, thick hair holds a side part well, and a low taper keeps the sides clean without too much skin showing. The longer top falls naturally to one side, so styling is as simple as a comb and a small amount of styling cream.

21. Swept-Up Ivy League with a Mustache

This Ivy League hairstyle keeps the sides short and leaves the top long enough to brush up and back. The graying sides look neat, and the lift on top balances out a full mustache. Use a little matte paste on dry hair and push it into place with your fingers.

22. Brushed-Up Ivy League with a Beard

The top of this Ivy League is cut short and brushed up, and the sides are faded close. Keeping the hair this short makes your beard the main focus. A small amount of clay is all you need to style it.

23. Ivy League with a Side Sweep

This is a classic Ivy League with the top combed smoothly to one side. The sides are tapered short to keep the shape clean. Comb a light pomade through damp hair for a neat, soft shine.

24. Textured Ivy League for Wavy Hair

Wavy hair does well with a little extra length up top, so the natural movement can show. Trimmed sides keep the cut from getting bushy as it grows. Scrunch a curl cream into damp hair, push the front loosely to one side, and leave it alone until it dries so the waves don’t frizz.

25. Short Ivy League for Gray Hair

Short gray hair can look sharp and modern when it has some direction, and this version pushes everything up and back with a little spike. Gray hair also looks crisp when the sides are kept short and close. Ask your stylist or barber for a number two on the sides and about an inch and a half on top to get this shape.

26. Curly Ivy League

Curls stay compact and defined when they’re kept short like this. A tapered back and sides give the haircut a clean outline. Plan on a trim every four to five weeks, since curly hair loses its shape fast once it gets too long.

27. Side-Parted Ivy League with Soft Waves

A clear side part gives this Ivy League structure, while gentle waves keep it from looking flat. Short, tapered sides make the hairstyle look clean. The waves add natural volume up top, so even medium-thick hair looks fuller with this shape.

28. Wavy Ivy League Haircut for Older Men

As hair turns gray, waves often become more noticeable, and this haircut makes good use of them. Leaving some length on top lets the waves form, while shorter sides keep things clean. The movement on top also helps disguise thinning spots, which makes it a smart pick if your hair isn’t as dense as it used to be.

29. Short Textured Ivy League Haircut

If you want the easiest version of an Ivy League as possible, this is it. A short, slightly messy top means there’s no part to keep up. The choppy texture hides cowlicks and uneven growth, so the cut still looks good a few weeks after your last trim.

30. Ivy League Haircut and Beard for Men Over 60

White hair looks full and healthy when it’s swept back and kept short on the sides. A trimmed white beard pairs well with the clean shape of this Ivy League. Keeping the beard about the same length as the sides helps the hair and beard blend into one smooth outline.

31. Slicked-Back Ivy League with a Mid Fade

Combing everything straight back gives this short haircut a sleek, sharp look. A mid fade on the sides adds contrast and keeps it clean. The fade starts to soften after about two weeks, so get regular touch-ups if you want to keep that crisp line.
